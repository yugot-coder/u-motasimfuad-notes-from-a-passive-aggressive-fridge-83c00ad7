One original passive-aggressive note a smart fridge leaves for its owner: a single dry, petty line, the sort of thing a smug appliance would put on its door screen.

> You opened me 47 times today and ate nothing. At this point, just move in — I charge less than your landlord.

## Why this line

- **Fridge's voice.** The joke is built on the fridge's own sensors: it counted the door opens (47) and the meals eaten (none). No other appliance could have written it.
- **Turn and sting.** The setup is a petty inventory report; the sting is the last clause — the fridge offers itself as a cheaper flatmate. The reader does not see the landlord line coming.
- **Small, domestic grudge.** Opening the door and eating nothing, a habit every owner recognises — not a rant or a threat.
- **Fresh, concrete detail.** A counted number, a specific habit, a rent joke — not generic nagging about eating vegetables.
- **Economy.** 109 characters, every word earning its place, nothing left to trim.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the line (109 characters, no trailing newline) to `<MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR>/entry.txt`
(default `/output/entry.txt`). Standard library only, deterministic, no network.
The program re-reads the file and asserts it is a single line of 1–200 characters
whose sha256 matches the line it intended to write.
